## 1. Business Identity

- **Name (English):** Jeunju Restaurant / JeunJu Korean Restaurant / "Jeun Ju" (spelling varies by platform — Yelp, Google, and press all use slightly different capitalization/spacing). One Yelp listing variant appears under "JEUNJUHEAHANGGUK," which likely reflects "Jeunju Haejangguk" (해장국 = hangover soup) — possibly an older or alternate registered name. *(via Yelp, Google/DineDroop, multiple aggregators)*
- **Name (Korean):** 전주식당 (Jeonju Sikdang, lit. "Jeonju Restaurant") and 전주할매칼국수 (Jeonju Halmae Kalguksu, lit. "Jeonju Grandma's Knife-Cut Noodles") *(via Google Business Profile/DineDroop)*
- **Owner:** **Sophia Cho** — referred to as "Sophie" in a 2017 feature and "Sophia" in 2023–2025 coverage. She is the daughter of the founder and now runs the restaurant. *(via Eat the World NYC 2017; Eater NY 2023; Homecoming NYC 2025)*
- **Founder / original head chef:** **Eunhae Bae**, Sophia's mother, originally from Jeonju, South Korea. *(via Eat the World NYC, 2017)*
- **Origin story:** Per Eater NY's Caroline Shin, Sophia took the restaurant over from her mother and has been working to continue her mother's approach while keeping on kitchen staff who have been there from the beginning — paraphrased from the Eater NY feature, not quoted directly. Per Homecoming NYC's event page, Sophia is described as building the restaurant into "a welcoming haven for lovers of traditional Korean food in Queens," driven by a personal connection to her Jeonju heritage. The restaurant's name is a direct tribute to the city of Jeonju, considered Korea's culinary capital and the birthplace of dishes like bibimbap. *(via Eater NY 2023; Homecoming NYC 2025)*
- **Year established:** Multiple sources (Facebook page tagline, aiprofile.com aggregator, QNS.com news coverage) state the restaurant has operated at this address **since 1999**, making it "nearly 40 years old" as described in 2025–2026 coverage. **However**, per Eat the World NYC's 2017 visit, the awning outside displays a photo of the founder/mother with "Since 1985" underneath it — the writer speculated this may not match the actual opening date of this specific location. **Flag: confirm the correct founding year and what "1985" refers to (possibly the mother's cooking background/an earlier establishment) directly with the owner before publishing a date on the new site.**
- **Tagline / mission statement:** Facebook page: *"Since 1999, we have been serving centuries-tested and -approved traditional homestyle recipes from Jeonju, the 'culinary capital' of South Korea."* (paraphrased close to source, short enough to summarize safely) *(via Facebook)*
- **Business category:** Korean restaurant / Asian restaurant, specializing in **Jeollado-region (Jeonju) home-style cooking** — soups, stews, and noodles — which multiple sources note distinguishes it from the many Korean BBQ-focused restaurants nearby. *(via Google Business attributes/DineDroop; Asian American Federation neighborhood guide, which categorizes it specifically as "Korean cuisine (Jeollado region)")*
- **Unique selling points emphasized across platforms:**
  - Authenticity and family lineage (mother-to-daughter recipes) — repeated across Yelp, Facebook, Eater NY, and Homecoming NYC
  - Generous portions and free banchan (side dish) refills — the single most repeated praise point across nearly every review source found
  - Vegetables sourced "straight from the family garden," per the owner-written Yelp "Specialties" section
  - Identifies as **Asian-owned** and **women-owned** *(via Google Business attributes/DineDroop)*
  - Longevity/neighborhood institution status — "nearly 40 years," "a Murray Hill gem" *(via QNS.com, Homecoming NYC)*

## 6. Voice & Tone

*(This section separates what the business has explicitly said about itself from Claude's read of the pattern, as requested.)*

**Explicitly stated by the business (Yelp "Specialties" section, written in first person by the owner/manager):** emphasizes "traditional, centuries-old Korean recipes," ingredients "carefully crafted from scratch," and vegetables sourced "straight from the family garden." Uses evocative, almost folk-remedy framing for signature dishes — describing gamjatang as filling "without feeling bloated or stuffed," and describing samgyetang with the traditional Korean concept of "fight fire with fire" (이열치열), tying the dish to the practice of eating hot soup during summer's hottest days.

**Explicitly stated (Facebook tagline):** "centuries-tested and -approved traditional homestyle recipes from Jeonju, the 'culinary capital' of South Korea" — leans on heritage and authenticity as the core promise.

**Explicitly stated (Instagram bio):** Plain and functional — "Enjoy homestyle Korean food at Jeunju Restaurant!" plus the address. No stylized voice; this channel reads as an afterthought rather than an active marketing channel (consistent with its very low follower/post count).

**Claude's observation on the overall pattern (not a direct quote from any one source):** Across the platforms that do have owner-written copy, the recurring vocabulary is *authentic, traditional, homestyle, family, centuries-old, generous.* The tone is warm and heritage-forward rather than polished or trend-driven — it reads like a family telling their own story, not a branding agency's copy. There's no irony, humor, or trendy slang anywhere in the business's own words; the voice is sincere and a little old-fashioned in a way that matches the "grandmother's kitchen" framing reviewers independently use. For site copy, this suggests leaning into the mother-daughter, Jeonju-heritage narrative directly and plainly, rather than writing something slicker or more ironic — a sleek visual presentation (per the Nubiani reference, below) paired with warm, plainspoken, heritage-first copy would likely fit this business better than copy that tries to sound trendy or hip.

## 8. Design & Style Reference — nubianinyc.com

*(Notes on the requested reference site, for later design use — this section is about the target aesthetic, not extracted JeunJu content.)*

Nubiani (a Manhattan Korean BBQ restaurant, two locations) uses a minimal, restaurant-forward layout:
- Simple top navigation: About / Location / Menu / Reservation — nothing more
- Large, full-bleed, high-quality food and interior photography carries most of the homepage; body copy is minimal and mostly restricted to a short tagline
- Clean, restrained serif/sans wordmark-style logo treatment; no cluttered graphics
- Reservations are handled through an external platform (Resy) linked directly from the nav, rather than a custom booking form
- Footer keeps contact minimal: Instagram handle, one email address, and both physical addresses
- Overall impression is quiet confidence — the photography and product (the food itself) are the design, rather than heavy branding elements or promotional copy

For JeunJu, replicating this structure (spare navigation, large photography, minimal copy) while swapping Nubiani's sleek/upscale-BBQ tone for JeunJu's warmer, more heritage/home-style voice (per §6) would likely capture what the client is asking for: a premium *presentation* wrapped around an authentically homey brand story, rather than a premium/upscale repositioning of the restaurant itself.
